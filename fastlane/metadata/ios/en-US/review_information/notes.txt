Screen recording: attached (iPhone, latest iOS, 1 min 40 s), from the app launch through the typical flow: saving a recipe under tags, finding it by one tag and by two, hosting a sync with a second phone, opening a record's details. There is no account, no content shared between users and no paid content, so none of those flows exist. Sync needs a second device on the same Wi-Fi; everything else works on one phone with no network, and the reviewer can skip it.

2. Purpose and audience: AIS Index is a personal, offline index. The user saves links, notes, file paths, commands and passwords under tags of their own choosing and finds them later by typing those tags. For anyone who wants to find their own things again without a cloud account. A search engine or an automatic tagger answers with what words mean to most people; the user needs what a word meant to them when they saved it. The index is plain text, stays on the phone and outlives the app.

3. Setup and access: nothing to set up and no credentials. Launch, tap +, type a value and one or more tags, Save. Type a tag in the search field to recall it; two tags return what was saved under both. Tags lists every tag used, Recent everything saved. The app starts with an empty index and the reviewer creates records in seconds.

4. External services: none. A C engine built into the binary and iOS system frameworks only. Voice input uses Apple's Speech framework with on-device recognition requested, so audio is not sent to a server. Sync, when the user chooses it, runs directly between two of the user's own devices on the same Wi-Fi, one showing a QR code and the other scanning it, end-to-end encrypted over the local network (hence NSLocalNetworkUsageDescription). A secret saved with the Encrypt switch is encrypted on the device with a passphrase the app never stores or transmits. No server, no analytics, no advertising, no payment processor, no AI service.

5. Regional differences: none. The app functions identically in every region where it is offered, with one listing in English.

6. Regulated industry or protected material: none. The app is the author's own open-source work (github.com/Anode1/ais, MIT licence for the App Store binary). Encryption uses standard algorithms; export compliance is answered as exempt.
